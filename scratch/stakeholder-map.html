Governance & Ecosystem

Every stakeholder Nervve touches, along five governance dimensions.

Rows are the parties that touch the platform — regulators, commercial counterparties, infrastructure providers, trust anchors, Nervve's own roles. Columns are the five dimensions along which any real grid-capacity marketplace must be governed. Each cell says what that stakeholder actually does under that dimension. Reads as a hiring guide, a regulator briefing appendix, and a RACI baseline — not decoration.

§ 01 Framing

What this document is, what it is not, and how it earns its keep alongside the technical docs.

The gap analysis, solo build plan, and target-state stack proposals tell you what Nervve is and how it is built. They say very little about who the platform accountably serves and answers to. This document fills that gap.

Five dimensions carry every consequential relationship the platform has with the outside world: information flow (what data moves in and out), control mechanisms (who is authorised to make what call), trading & clearing (who plays what role in a live trade), audit trail (who reads the record and how far back), and cybersecurity (who owns what control and answers when it fails). Every stakeholder shows up on at least three of these dimensions; a few show up on all five. That's the point.

Use it as: (a) a hiring guide — every dimension is a role Nervve is either performing itself or paying someone else to perform; (b) a regulator briefing appendix — the EMA sandbox conversation goes better when you can point to a page that names every party in the room; and (c) a RACI baseline — the last section formalises decision authority for the six most consequential operational processes.

§ 02 The five dimensions

Each dimension has a colour used in the matrix below and the diagram that follows. Together they cover every non-trivial interaction the platform has with the outside world.

01 · Information flow

What data moves.

Every feed, publication, and notification — meter readings inbound, dispatch signals outbound, invoices to counterparties, reports to regulators.

02 · Control mechanisms

Who authorises what.

Sign-off, licensing, market-participation authority, deploy authority, dispute adjudication, break-glass overrides — where authority actually sits.

03 · Trading & clearing

Who plays what role in a trade.

Buyer, seller, clearing operator, delivery agent, settlement rail, market referee — the classical exchange roles, applied to a grid-capacity marketplace.

04 · Audit trail

Who reads the record.

Who has visibility into which events, over what retention window, and via what verification path (WORM logs, HSM-signed Merkle roots, TSA timestamps).

05 · Cybersecurity

Who owns what control.

CII designation, target-architecture ownership, incident response, monitoring, attestation, PDPA obligations — and who answers when a control fails.

§ 06 Notes for the founder

A few observations that are more useful as prose than as table cells.

Every empty cell is a hidden liability. When a row's dimension shows n/a, sanity-check that the party genuinely has no role — not that someone forgot to ask. Empty cells that should not be empty are how governance gaps become regulator findings.

Nervve is deliberately small on the trading row. The platform is the clearing house, not a counterparty. Recuse the Founder / CEO from any trade whose counterparties they personally know until an in-house Compliance officer exists. This is not paranoia; it is the standard governance a market operator answers for.

The four "primary" borders in the ecosystem view are the four hires you cannot postpone. Corten border (Founder / CEO) exists. Gold-bordered nodes (Capacity Holders, Buyers, SP Group, Bank/PSP) are already lined up by the pilot. Crimson-bordered nodes (CISO/DPO, Retained SOC, CSA counterpart) become active in Phase 2 — retain the CISO now, contract the SOC before the first live dispatch, engage CSA the moment the pilot has real counterparties.

The Advisory board is not a control node. They advise, they do not authorise. Every row on the RACI table has exactly one A; the Advisory board never holds it. If a board member starts making calls, either promote them into an executive role or clarify the boundary. Both are fine outcomes; the mixed state is not.
